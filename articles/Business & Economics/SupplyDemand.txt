📊 UNDERSTANDING SUPPLY AND DEMAND

🌱 Introduction

Why do vegetable prices sometimes increase during heavy rainfall? Why do popular mobile phones sell quickly? Why might a shop reduce the price of products that remain unsold?

These situations can be explained through two important economic concepts: supply and demand.

Supply and demand describe the relationship between the quantity of a product available in the market and the quantity customers are willing to purchase.

🛍️ What Is Demand?

Demand refers to the quantity of a product or service that consumers are willing and able to purchase at different prices during a particular period.

Generally, when the price of a product decreases, more people may be willing to buy it. When the price increases, some customers may reduce their purchases.

For example, imagine a notebook costs ₹100. Many students may be willing to purchase it. If the price increases to ₹200, some students might choose a cheaper alternative.

This relationship is known as the law of demand, assuming other factors remain unchanged.

Demand is influenced by several factors, including income, customer preferences, population, product quality, and the prices of related products.

🏭 What Is Supply?

Supply refers to the quantity of a product or service that producers are willing and able to sell at different prices during a particular period.

Generally, producers may be willing to supply more products when prices increase because higher prices can make production more profitable.

For example, if the market price of mangoes rises, farmers and sellers may have a greater incentive to bring more mangoes to the market.

This relationship is known as the law of supply, assuming other factors remain unchanged.

Supply can be affected by production costs, technology, weather conditions, taxes, availability of raw materials, and the number of producers.

⚖️ Understanding Market Equilibrium

Market equilibrium occurs when the quantity demanded by consumers equals the quantity supplied by producers at a particular price.

At this point, the market has no overall shortage or surplus under the assumed conditions.

Imagine that customers want to purchase 100 bottles of juice at ₹20 each, and sellers are willing to supply exactly 100 bottles at that price.

The quantity demanded and quantity supplied are equal. Therefore, ₹20 is the equilibrium price in this simplified example.

📈 What Happens When Demand Increases?

Suppose a new movie makes a particular snack extremely popular. More people begin purchasing it.

If supply does not increase immediately, demand may become greater than supply.

This creates a shortage, and sellers may increase prices.

A similar situation can happen when a product becomes popular during festivals or when a new electronic device is released.

📉 What Happens When Supply Increases?

Imagine that a fruit harvest is particularly successful. More fruits become available in the market.

If customer demand remains unchanged, the increased supply may put downward pressure on prices.

A surplus can occur when sellers offer more products than customers are willing to purchase at the current price.

Businesses may respond by reducing prices, offering discounts, or changing production levels.

🌦️ A Real-World Example of Vegetable Prices

Consider tomato prices.

During a period of good weather, farmers may produce a large quantity of tomatoes. The market receives more supply, which can help keep prices lower.

However, heavy rainfall or transportation problems may reduce the quantity reaching markets.

If customer demand remains similar while supply falls, prices may increase.

This example shows how natural events and transportation conditions can influence everyday prices.

💻 Supply and Demand in the Digital World

Supply and demand also operate in technology markets.

When a new smartphone becomes highly popular but production is limited, demand may exceed available supply.

Streaming platforms, online courses, cloud services, and software subscriptions also respond to customer demand.

Businesses study market trends to estimate how many products customers may purchase and how much they should produce.

✨ Final Thought

Supply and demand are not just textbook concepts. They help explain why prices change, why products become popular, and how businesses decide what to produce.

🔑 KEY TAKEAWAY

Supply represents what producers offer, while demand represents what consumers want and can purchase. Their interaction influences market prices and the availability of goods and services.
